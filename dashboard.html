Tab pause

Extra destinations are kept safely on a pause page. Set 0 to observe tab patterns without starting a cooldown.

Mouse coach

The coach first shows a quiet awareness cue. After several uncertain mouse patterns close together, it asks you to name your next action and complete a short hands-off reset. It never reads what you type on websites.

Rabbit-hole checkpoint

When several hyperlinks form one trail within 20 minutes, pause the page and ask what you want to do next. Reload and Back do not deepen the trail. Paths and link text are not stored. Turning the pause off keeps the aggregate trail count available in your dashboard.

Scrolling and infinite feeds

After enough active downward scrolling, freeze the page, ask for intent, and require the hands-off reset. This can flag a habit loop; it cannot determine whether you are tired. Turning the pause off keeps aggregate scroll checkpoints available.

After ten substantial new images or videos load near the viewport following real scrolling, the feed stays locked until both drift questions contain at least 50 words combined. A hands-off reset follows. An ordinary reload restores an unanswered checkpoint in that tab. Turning this off keeps only the aggregate count.

History insights
Learn recurring, low-continuity detours

With optional history access, Behavyour groups the last 30 days into website episodes. It looks for returns to an interrupted site, brief exits, repeat checks, recurrence across days, and the number of different contexts interrupted. Analysis stays here.

Chrome’s warning says “read and change history” because that is the browser’s generic permission label. Behavyour calls only the read methods; disabling this feature revokes the permission and clears its derived results.

Important limit: browser history has navigation timestamps but no tab focus, closing time, scrolling, or proof of intent. “Brief” means the next different-site visit appeared within two minutes; it is an estimate, not measured attention or a diagnosis. How to read these patterns

Mouse pattern: substantial circling, back-and-forth movement, or an uncertain click with no clear page action. Tab shift: an extra tab was paused, or a categorized page was left again quickly. On-page checkpoint: a hyperlink trail, sustained downward-scrolling session, or continuing batch of ten new feed visuals crossed its threshold. A feed lock requires both drift answers and at least 50 words combined. Recovery action: you completed a hands-off reset, returned to the earlier task, or saved a tab for later. These are imperfect observations, not a diagnosis or a grade.
